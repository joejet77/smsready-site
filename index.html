Questions operators ask

I'm a single-pilot operation. Doesn't the rule mostly exempt me?

Only if you're a "sole individual" — one person who does everything: flies, schedules, plans, fuels, handles the airplane, and tracks and coordinates maintenance. A second pilot, a scheduler, or letting your shop track maintenance means the full rule applies. Even a true sole individual still owes risk management, most of safety assurance, training, documentation, and the declaration. The gap check tells you which you are and why.

Can't I just use the FAA's free WBAT tool?

WBAT is a reporting database. The FAA says it "can be used to assist" — compliance is the implemented processes and the records, not the software. You'd still need the policy, the risk process, the reviews, the training records, and the paragraph-by-paragraph mapping. SMS Ready includes WBAT-compatible reporting if you want to keep using it.

Do I need an SMS manual?

No. Part 5 requires documented policy, processes, and procedures. The FAA's advisory circular says a compliance statement mapping each paragraph to your documents is acceptable. We generate that statement; the "manual" is a set of short, usable documents.

What does the FAA do with my declaration?

Acknowledges it. There's no approval step — the declaration is your legal statement that the SMS is developed and implemented. Inspectors verify each component in surveillance afterward, which is why the evidence binder matters more than the letter.

Does this guarantee I pass?

No one can promise that, and anyone who does is selling you something else. SMS Ready gives you a complete, running system and the records to show for it. Compliance stays with the certificate holder and its accountable executive, exactly as the rule says.
